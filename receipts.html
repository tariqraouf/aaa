The Record

The Receipts.

Apple wants you to believe it's on the side of the rebels. Here's what it actually did.

For decades, Apple has built itself up as a company that strives to “make the world a better place” — from its 1984 Super Bowl commercial claiming to fight Big Brother, to its “think different” campaign. This company wants you to believe it's on the side of the rebels — the ones crazy enough to change the world.

But over the last few decades, it has proven just as evil, greedy, and harmful as any other massive tech corporation. It has fired employees for standing with Palestine, ignored the pleas of its Arab and Muslim employees asking for empathy, and cozied up to Donald Trump, normalizing this administration's fascist ideas and policies.

Below are just a few examples of the ways Apple has supported Israel, ignored the people of Congo, and brown-nosed fascism.

Receipts 01

Zionism

Acquisitions, IDF pay, executive visits, matched donations, and offices on occupied land.

$34.5MReported employee donations matched to Friends of the IDF and settlement-linked groups

January 2026 · Acquisitions

Apple is no stranger to buying up Israeli tech companies, many started by former Unit 8200 soldiers. In January 2026, Apple acquired Israeli AI startup Q.ai for roughly $1.6–2 billion — its second-largest acquisition ever, after Beats.

TechCrunch ↗

Since 2024 · Internal HR policy

According to Apple's own internal HR site, workers who serve in the IDF are paid 100% of their base salary, with IDF spouses offered an extra 8 days off since 2024.

Source: Apple's internal HR site, as reported by employees.

April 2025 · Executive delegation

Apple SVP of Hardware Technologies Johny Srouji led a senior executive delegation to Apple's R&D centers in Herzliya, Haifa, and Jerusalem.

Globes ↗

Donation matching · Benevity

Apple's Benevity donation-matching program has matched employee contributions — reported at $34.5 million — to Friends of the IDF and West Bank settlement-linked groups.

The Intercept ↗

Occupied Jerusalem · Office listings

Employees allege Apple lists its Jerusalem offices as being in Israel rather than occupied territory — despite the ICJ's July 2024 ruling that the occupation is unlawful.

Receipts 02

Supply Chain

Conflict minerals, a sourcing retreat, and a pending lawsuit.

5 of 7of Rwanda's largest coltan exporters bought conflict coltan from Rubaya, per Global Witness

June 2024 – November 2025 · Sourcing & lawsuit

Apple disclosed that in June 2024 it directed suppliers to stop sourcing tin, tantalum, tungsten, and gold directly or indirectly from the DRC and Rwanda, citing escalating conflict, smuggling, and illegal taxation. In November 2025, International Rights Advocates filed a District of Columbia consumer-protection lawsuit challenging Apple's responsible-sourcing claims.

The lawsuit remains pending; its allegations have not been adjudicated, and Apple disputes them.

Apple SEC filing ↗IRAdvocates ↗

2026 · Global Witness investigation

A 2026 Global Witness investigation found that at least five of Rwanda's seven largest coltan exporters had bought conflict coltan from the Rubaya mines in eastern Congo. The investigation identified pathways through traders and smelters by which that material may reach global technology supply chains, including Apple's.

This shows a serious supply-chain risk, but it does not prove that a particular Apple product contains Rubaya coltan.
