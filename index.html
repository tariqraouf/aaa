We Are Apples Against Apartheid.

A collective of current and former Apple employees bringing attention to Apple's complicity in Israel's apartheid system.

We create campaigns designed to bring attention to Apple's anti-Palestinian racism and Islamophobia. We also work with Friends of Congo, Team Congo, and other Congolese activists to expose how abusive and opaque mineral supply chains harm Congolese communities — and to demand transparency, accountability, and responsible sourcing that benefits Congo's people.

Our Demands.

Four things Apple must do to say no to blood tech.

Demand 01

Research, Report, Remediate

Apple's business practices have harmed marginalized communities all over the world. If it truly wants to “make the world a better place,” it will research that harm, report its findings publicly, and create a plan to meaningfully remediate it.

Demand 02

Listen to the Community

Meet with members of the Palestinian and Congolese communities and develop a long-term consultation process to determine corporate policies and activities in the DRC, Israel and Palestine.

Demand 03

Get Out of the Occupation

Apple lists offices in occupied Jerusalem as being in Israel, against international law. Close them, and stop including organizations that fund and arm illegal settlements on the Benevity platform.

Demand 04

Clean the Supply Chain, Don't Abandon Congo

Keep minerals controlled, taxed, or trafficked by armed groups out of the supply chain. Publish mineral origins, processors and smelters, remediate harms, and support responsible sourcing from legitimate Congolese producers.

Zionism

In January 2026 Apple acquired Israeli AI startup Q.ai for roughly $1.6–2 billion — its second-largest acquisition ever, after Beats. Source ↗

According to Apple's own internal HR site, workers who serve in the IDF are paid 100% of their base salary, with IDF spouses offered an extra 8 days off since 2024.

Apple's Benevity donation-matching program has matched employee contributions — reported at $34.5 million — to Friends of the IDF and West Bank settlement-linked groups. Source ↗

Supply Chain

Apple disclosed that in June 2024 it directed suppliers to stop sourcing tin, tantalum, tungsten, and gold from the DRC and Rwanda. In November 2025, International Rights Advocates filed a lawsuit challenging Apple's responsible-sourcing claims; it remains pending, and Apple disputes it. Source ↗

A 2026 Global Witness investigation found at least five of Rwanda's seven largest coltan exporters bought conflict coltan from the Rubaya mines in eastern Congo. Source ↗
