For Congo and Palestine.

Since 2012, Apple has embraced Netanyahu's apartheid state — ignoring human rights violations recognized by Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International, even after the genocidal attacks on Gaza began. It has ignored the Congolese communities most affected by the demand for its products.

Employees who support Palestine have been reprimanded, suspended, and fired. On October 23, launch day for iPhone Duo, we call on communities everywhere to picket their local Apple Store. We'll provide the flyers. Just show up.

Our Demands.

Four things Apple must do to say no to blood tech.

Demand 01

Research, Report, Remediate

Apple's business practices have harmed marginalized communities all over the world. If it truly wants to “make the world a better place,” it will research that harm, report its findings publicly, and create a plan to meaningfully remediate it.

Demand 02

Listen to the Community

Meet with members of the Palestinian and Congolese communities and develop a long-term consultation process to determine corporate policies and activities in the DRC, Israel and Palestine.

Demand 03

Get Out of the Occupation

Apple lists offices in occupied Jerusalem as being in Israel, against international law. Close them, and stop including organizations that fund and arm illegal settlements on the Benevity platform.

Demand 04

Clean the Supply Chain, Don't Abandon Congo

Keep minerals controlled, taxed, or trafficked by armed groups out of the supply chain. Publish mineral origins, processors and smelters, remediate harms, and support responsible sourcing from legitimate Congolese producers.

Receipts 01

Zionism

Apple is no stranger to buying Israeli tech companies, many started by former Unit 8200 soldiers. In January 2026 it acquired Israeli AI startup Q.ai for roughly $1.6–2 billion — its second-largest acquisition ever, after Beats. Source ↗

According to Apple's own internal HR site, workers who serve in the IDF are paid 100% of their base salary, with IDF spouses offered an extra 8 days off since 2024.

In April 2025, Apple SVP of Hardware Technologies Johny Srouji led a senior executive delegation to Apple's R&D centers in Herzliya, Haifa, and Jerusalem. Source ↗

Apple's Benevity donation-matching program has matched employee contributions — reported at $34.5 million — to Friends of the IDF and West Bank settlement-linked groups. Source ↗

Employees allege Apple lists its Jerusalem offices as being in Israel rather than occupied territory — despite the ICJ's July 2024 ruling that the occupation is unlawful. Source ↗

Receipts 02

Supply Chain

Apple disclosed that in June 2024 it directed suppliers to stop sourcing tin, tantalum, tungsten, and gold directly or indirectly from the DRC and Rwanda, citing escalating conflict, smuggling, and illegal taxation. In November 2025, International Rights Advocates filed a District of Columbia consumer-protection lawsuit challenging Apple's responsible-sourcing claims. The lawsuit remains pending; its allegations have not been adjudicated, and Apple disputes them. Source 1 ↗ Source 2 ↗

A 2026 Global Witness investigation found that at least five of Rwanda's seven largest coltan exporters had bought conflict coltan from the Rubaya mines in eastern Congo, and identified pathways through traders and smelters by which that material may reach global technology supply chains, including Apple's. This shows a serious supply-chain risk, but does not prove that a particular Apple product contains Rubaya coltan. Source ↗

Receipts 03

Bootlicking

Apple is one of the corporate donors funding Trump's $300 million White House ballroom project, alongside Amazon, Google, Meta, and Microsoft. Source ↗

In August 2025, Tim Cook gave Trump a custom glass plaque mounted on a 24-karat gold base during an Oval Office event announcing a $100 billion Apple investment in the U.S. — widely read as an effort to secure tariff exemptions. Source ↗
